Nonlocal curvature

Classical curvature κ(z) at a point z measures the curve’s infinitesimal bending there: for an arc-length parametrization, κ = |dT/ds| — the magnitude of the rate of change of the unit tangent (equivalently κ = |γ′ × γ″|/|γ′|3 in any parametrization), pointing along the principal normal.

Instead of the infinitesimal neighborhood, nonlocal curvature κσ(z) integrates over the (2n−2)-dimensional space of disks in ℝn: every point of the domain represents a disk D of radius r with a given center offset and orientation. Each disk’s contribution is weighted by r(−1−σ), multiplied by the parity of the disk’s intersection with the curve (+1 for an even number of intersections, −1 for odd). Notice this emphasizes small disks. The result is a vector at z that sees the whole curve.

This tool (the ℝ3 case)

This tool implements the case n = 3: curves in three-space, with a 4-dimensional integration domain (radius + disk position/orientation). The integral is evaluated by Monte Carlo — the r-integral splits at a singularity radius rs into a near-field head (fixed-radius sampling, linear extrapolation to r = 0) and a far-field tail (Pareto sampling), with disk parameters sampled uniformly.

The point of the tool is the σ → 1 limit: the rescaled vector κ̃σ := (1−σ)κσ/c3, with c3 = π2√2 ≈ 13.96, converges to the classical curvature vector as σ → 1. That convergence is the subject of Dan Zimmerman’s paper “The asymptotics of nonlocal curvature for curves.” The paper operates in full generality rather than focusing on ℝ3, but since we live in three dimensions it’s easiest to visualize ℝ3.

The main 3D view shows the curve, the evaluation point z, and the classical vs. rescaled nonlocal curvature vectors side by side. The σ-sweep plot traces |κ̃σ| against σ, with the exact circle formula and the κ(z) limit line for reference.

Tuning the Monte Carlo parameters
  • Nhead: MC samples in the head part — the fixed-radius estimate of F(rs). More samples → smaller SE on the near-singularity contribution, linear time cost.
  • Ntail: Pareto samples in the tail (r > rs). More samples → smaller SE on the far-field contribution.
  • rs (singularity radius): the split point between head and tail. Smaller rs makes the head's linear extrapolation F(r) ≈ [F(rs)/rs]·r on (0, rs) more accurate but shifts more of the integral into the tail; larger rs puts more into the cheap fixed-radius head sampling, but the linear extrapolation degrades if F curves appreciably on (0, rs). Rule of thumb: keep rs small next to the curve's radius of curvature; if one part's SE dominates the other, shift rs or rebalance Nhead vs Ntail.
  • Error-bar scaling: SE ∼ 1/√N, so 4× samples halves the error bars.
These take effect on the next Compute; changing rs marks current results stale.

Conventions & caveats κσ(z) = limε→0 [ ∫Ae⁺ − ∫Ao⁺ ] (b∧a)t(z) / r1+σ dH⁴
  • Sign. Implemented as [Ae⁺ − Ao⁺], Dan's definition (1.8): for the counterclockwise unit circle at z=(1,0,0) this gives κσ along −x̂ (inward), parallel to the classical curvature vector. Seguin's eq. (109) writes the sign-uncorrected odd−even order; this page flips it so the displayed κσ (orange) matches Dan's convention, and the 3D view shows the classical κ⃗ vector (green) beside it.
  • Measure convention. dH⁴(a,b,r) is the Hausdorff measure of U⊥²(ℝ³) with the metric induced from ℝ⁶, i.e. √2 dS²(a) dS¹a(b) dr: a uniform on S², b uniform on the circle perpendicular to a, times √2. The MC constants carry this √2.
  • Odd-only numerics. The vector function v=(b∧a)t = b(a·t)−a(b·t) has zero mean over all discs under a↔−a, so with the [even−odd] sign the even term never needs separate MC: F(r) = −2·∫odd v dμ. This is exactly the same expectation with far less noise.
  • Open-disk counting. Each disc is the open disk D(z+a·r,b,r) = {z+a·r+ξv : ξ∈[0,r)}: a polyline-to-plane crossing counts only when strictly inside the disk (|q−c|<r); crossings on the rim, including z itself, never count. Counts test as odd/even over the true open-disk curve intersection, not boundary intersections.
  • Osculating window at z. Counts near z use the osculating parabola z+s·T(z)+s²/2·κ̂(z), exact to O(h³), instead of the polyline chord: when z is an exact vertex the two adjacent segments are jointly accounted; otherwise the single z-bracketing segment is. Excluding the z-segment would lose ~7% of F(r) at defaults (O((h/r)0.8)), while counting it as a chord injects a ~40× parity artifact when z is off-vertex (interpolated sagitta crossing lands inside the disc).
  • Singularity treatment. κσ ≈ HEAD + TAIL: HEAD linearly extrapolates F(r) ≈ [F(rs)/rs]·r on (0, rs) via fixed-r MC at rs; TAIL uses Pareto MC, r = rs(1−u)−1/σ. A bare ε-cutoff would wrongly give (1−σ)κσ→0 as σ→1 and is not used. Head bias is O(rs).
  • Normalization. (1−σ)κσ → c₃·κ n̂ with c₃ = π²√2 ≈ 13.9577 (Zimmerman, The asymptotics of nonlocal curvature for curves, Theorem 6.1). Everywhere above this is displayed as the normalized vector κ̃σ := (1−σ)κσ/c₃, which converges directly to the classical curvature vector κ n̂ as σ→1. The results table's "c₃ est" row measures |F(rs)|/(rs·κ) directly against this constant. On the unit circle, exact finite-σ values come from Corollary 4.2 (drawn as the gray dash-dot sweep curve, divided by c₃).
  • Degenerate discs. Discs with a curve endpoint strictly inside the disc (open curves only) are skipped; endpoints on the rim are measure-zero and handled by strict inequalities.
  • Monte Carlo uncertainty. SE = standard error of the estimator mean. Warns when relative SE > 20%.
  • σ-sweep plot. The cyan points are measured |κ̃σ| at 14 σ values spanning [σmin, σmax] (denser toward σmax, N=20k tail per point, seed-fixed); their error bars are the Monte Carlo SE of |κ̃σ| at each point, propagated from the head and tail sample variances via SE(|v|)≈|û·ε| with û the mean direction (the head estimate is cached from the last Compute). The gray dash-dot curve (circle preset only) is Dan's exact finite-σ Corollary 4.2 value divided by c₃. The orange dashed line is κ(z), the classical curvature magnitude at the current evaluation point — the σ→1 limit of |κ̃σ| (Theorem 6.1), redrawn whenever t₀ or the curve changes.
  • Discrete bookkeeping. Disc∩curve counts use the polyline (plus the osculating window at z), not the true curve; raise the polyline resolution for strongly bending custom curves.
